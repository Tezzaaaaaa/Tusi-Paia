A bilingual reading

Same opening claim,
different grammar.

English is the King James Version; Samoan is the Samoan Edited Old Version (SOV, 2017). They are not word-for-word equivalents.

ENGLISH · KING JAMES VERSION“In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth.”Genesis 1:1 · KJV, 1611 translation (modern spelling)
GAGANA SAMOA · SOV“Na faia e le Atua le lagi ma le lalolagi i le amataga.”Kenese 1:1 · Bible Society of the South Pacific, 2017

English opens with “In the beginning”; the Samoan sentence places the action “Na faia” first and closes with “i le amataga”. Translation choices reflect grammar and style, not simple substitution.

The Samoan story

The Bible in Sāmoa

The Samoan Bible emerged through translation, printing, revision and local collaboration. Its history should not be reduced to the work of one missionary or one date — and “the Samoan Bible” does not refer to a single fixed wording.

Read the Samoan story →

  1. 1830s

    Translation work begins

    London Missionary Society missionaries begin translating biblical books into Samoan, with several translators involved.

  2. 1841

    Printed portions

    A Samoan Gospel of John is printed, followed by other portions.

  3. 1862

    First complete Samoan Bible

    Identified by the Reed Gallery's bibliographic account; some accounts give 1860. The guide notes the discrepancy rather than hiding it.

  4. 1884

    A lasting revision

    The LMS revision becomes the basis for later reprints.

  5. 2009 → PRESENT

    Contemporary Samoan

    The Contemporary Samoan New Testament is completed in 2009; work toward a modern Old Testament continues.

Accuracy and scope

Evidence first.

A short educational guide, not a complete Bible text or a critical edition.

History, not doctrine

Historical evidence is kept separate from theological interpretation, and communities' own understanding of the texts is respected.

Honest about uncertainty

Where scholarship or catalogues disagree, such as 1860 versus 1862, the page says so instead of presenting false certainty.

Named editions

Every passage names its edition, so you always know exactly which translation you are reading.
